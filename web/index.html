Method and limits

For every aircraft, readsb measures the received power (RSSI, dBFS). Correcting it for free-space path loss (FSPL, 20 log d + 20 log f + 32.44) leaves the antenna gain in the direction of the aircraft, plus the transponder's own power.

Per-aircraft correction: each transponder's own contribution (125 to 500 W, cabling, antenna) is estimated by alternating least squares over all its positions, then removed. The mean level is kept, so two campaigns can be compared in absolute terms.

Limits: at long range only strong enough signals are decoded, which slightly overestimates the gain at very low elevations; RSSI saturates above −3 dBFS (those samples are dropped); elevation depends on the antenna altitude you entered. Each displayed cell holds at least 5 samples (median).

Sector diagnosis

The measured mask is the elevation below which no aircraft are received (2nd percentile). If it is clearly above the terrain mask, the obstruction is local: building, roof, wall, mast. Without low traffic in a direction, the measured mask is overestimated.

RF profile

200°

"Apparent" heights: Earth curvature (radio radius, k = 4/3) is removed, so radio waves travel in straight lines. The grazing ray leaves the antenna and touches the highest obstacle; the orange band is the first Fresnel zone (17.3 √(d₁d₂ / (f·d)) m at 1090 MHz): an obstacle that cuts into it already attenuates the signal.

Measurement campaigns

A campaign marks one antenna setup ("bare dipole", "with reflector"…). Start it once the antenna is in place, stop it before changing the installation. Its samples are kept with no time limit.

Settings

Ground altitude plus mast height. It is used to compute each aircraft's elevation angle and the radio horizon. Elevation is recomputed at analysis time, so a correction also applies to samples already collected.
